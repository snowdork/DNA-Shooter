# Product vision and game design document

## One-sentence pitch
**Every shot changes a possibility. Every generation tests it. Every surviving lineage tells an evolutionary story.**

## Product promise
A satisfying neon 1980s-style 2D arcade shooter that uses scientifically informed genetics and population selection to turn moment-to-moment action into long-term evolutionary consequences. The player does not freely invent predetermined lifeforms. They experiment with variation, see results unfold in different environments, and learn from outcomes.

## Four pillars
1. **Immediate arcade feel** — precise movement; exciting collision, sound, combos and boss events; readable targets and high frame rate.
2. **Cause and consequence** — every important DNA event is recorded and may affect offspring, selection, or nothing observable.
3. **Scientific integrity** — heritability, drift, selection and ecological context matter; never misrepresent science as literal shooting inside a nucleus.
4. **Replayable discovery** — save experiment state, branch at a past generation, compare alternate populations, collect lineage histories.

## Modes
### DNA Battle
Stacked 2D shooter: rendered animal above, colorful horizontal wiggling DNA in the middle, and shooter/healer/mutator stations in green muck below. A top title bar and bottom scoring HUD frame the action. Supported modeled traits drive the animal preview; unresolved changes are labeled pending. The player fires different tools; enemy hazards (free radicals, repair deadlines, unstable fragments) create gameplay pressure. The double helix animation is a stylized UI for a small chosen genomic window.

### Evolution Lab
Zoom into the genome event. Display gene region, base change, possible coding/regulatory effect, resulting modeled trait and uncertainty, and a lesson in plain language. Inspect population distributions of traits and source provenance.

### Living World
Observe or intervene in the environment: seasonal cold, food scarcity, migration, predators, and reproduction; advance a configurable number of generations. Display allele frequencies, survival and lineage branching. No single individual transforms through its lifetime into a different species.

## Target audience
Curious teens, adults, arcade players, amateur biologists, and STEM educators. Accessible beginner explanations with optional deeper gene, inheritance, and population-genetics inspection.

## Player goals
- Survive levels with enough energy and DNA repair capacity.
- Build viable and sometimes adaptive genetic variation, not 'always beneficial mutations'.
- Keep populations from extinction under shifting conditions.
- Understand why the same variant can be favorable, neutral, or harmful in different contexts.
- Discover documented genetic examples and explore founder effects, genetic drift, recombination, and eventually speciation.
- Branch and compare controlled 'what-if' simulations from a common seed.

## Not in scope for MVP
- Predicting novel viable animals from arbitrarily edited full genomes.
- Fully realistic molecular dynamics, clinical interpretation, or laboratory editing procedures.
- Instant invention of wings/gills/horns from single edits.
- Multiplayer, massive taxonomic catalog, whole-genome loading in-browser by default.

## Success measures
- A first-time player understands controls and 'what changed' within ~2 minutes.
- One complete arcade-to-inheritance-to-selection cycle takes roughly 8–12 minutes.
- Replay with same inputs and seed produces the same experiment history.
- Educational explanations distinguish verified facts from simplified/invented gameplay.
- Runs smoothly on typical desktop browsers and can degrade effects on low-powered devices.

## World and long-term direction
Begin with *Drosophila melanogaster*. Extend to zebrafish, mice, lizards, and other species via curated modules. A labeled Speculative Evolution mode can offer visually extravagant alternate life forms, clearly separated from source-backed real genetics.
