# Playable ten-minute scenario — "Winter of the Fruit Fly"

**Premise:** A population of fruit flies occupies a sheltered orchard environment. Its food sources and predator pressure will change, and the player experiments with a small *documented* locus.

## Minute 0–1: Pick the experiment
Player starts from 100 model individuals, a fixed seed and one curated chromosome window. UI introduces wild-type/reference DNA and paired bases. The scenario informs players that the shooter view is stylized.

## Minute 1–3: Arcade encounter
Player selects shooter, healer or mutator from three stations in green muck and aims upward at a colorful horizontal wiggling helix. The current fruit fly is rendered above, with unresolved changes marked pending. The player fires proton blasts (unpredictable DNA damage) and later a limited base-editor charge at identified targets; repair mitigates integrity damage. Events feed a panel: 'Damage repaired — no permanent variant', 'Variant detected — consequence unknown', or 'Curated variant applied — model effect to inspect'.

## Minute 3–4: Lab reveal
The viewer highlights an inherited or noninherited event and explains why. For a **specific curated** locus, the game may show a modeled change in visible pigmentation or another sourced trait; otherwise it says unknown/no clear visible consequence. It compares alleles in a diploid organism.

## Minute 4–7: Generations
Advance 5–20 modeled generations. An environment shifts food availability and predator conditions. Selection and drift influence relative reproductive success. Player watches distributions rather than watching an individual morph dramatically.

## Minute 7–9: Branch the result
Player splits the saved simulation into two alternate environmental histories using the same initial seed and variation. One population may thrive while another declines, or chance may cause both to lose a newly introduced allele.

## Minute 9–10: Discovery and replay
Show the annotated event timeline, changes in allele frequency and outcomes across branches. Award a scientific achievement for successfully interpreting a neutral or lost allele, not only for 'winning evolution'. Invite the next controlled experiment.

## Educational questions surfaced naturally
- Was the shot only DNA damage, or did it persist as a mutation?
- Was it present in the reproductive lineage?
- Did the mutation alter a modeled trait?
- Did environment-specific fitness favor it?
- Did genetic drift overpower that advantage in a small population?
- Could repeated runs produce different outcomes with different seeds?

## First release mission adjustment
The 'winter' environment is a gameplay experiment for flies, **not** a claim that changing their pigmentation automatically grants snow camouflage. If a trait-environment relationship is not sourced, label its fitness mapping as a hypothetical simplified model.
